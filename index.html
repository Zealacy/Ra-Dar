Station readings table
Regional snow stations (SNOTEL) · not used in the score

These federal NRCS stations are reliable, but they are not at Mt. Bachelor. They sit 11–18 miles away, at 4,770–5,680 ft, in different drainages, so they often get far less snow than the ski area. Use them only for the regional trend.

SWE is snow water equivalent. Density is ΔSWE ÷ Δdepth: under 7% is light, dry powder, 7–12% is typical, and over 12% is heavy, wet snow.

How accurate is this?

Days 1–3: generally reliable. Timing and amounts can still shift, especially for snow, which is the hardest thing to forecast in the mountains.

Days 4–5: good for the overall pattern (stormy or dry, warm or cold). Exact snow totals often change.

Days 6–7: trends only. Use the ranges and confidence, not the single numbers.

The range shows where 80% of 82 slightly different model runs land (the American GEFS and European ECMWF ensembles). A wide range or low confidence means the models disagree, so expect the forecast to change. Snow here is for mid-mountain (7,800 ft); the summit usually gets more and the base less. Check back daily: forecasts update every few hours.
